# The living grimoire

Original fantasy magic inspired by the user's Gardner Village/Salem trip, not a reconstruction of real places or historical witchcraft.

Three spells are available immediately. Witchlight sends a golden thread toward an optional room witch mark. Wildbloom grows four to eight modeled flowers as the saved atlas fills. Starweave connects nine stars into an airborne constellation. F and a touch button cast the prepared spell; the grimoire prepares another. Controller users can choose spells through the grimoire's ordinary modal choices. Casting has a 3.5-second active-time cooldown, no puzzle-resource cost, and no effect on haunting answers or Ninth Door seals.

Suitable rooms have one deterministic optional mark. Its position is inside the room, separated from quest and elevator points, clear of furniture, and connected to a certified standing point by a sampled clear segment. Marks are revealed for 24 active seconds by nearby Witchlight, then recorded by a physical interaction. Eight kinds of lore page persist under an independent versioned storage key; existing game identities and saves are unchanged. Claimed marks stay softly visible when revisiting. Storage failure preserves the current session without claiming durability.

Room actions answer with short spell signatures. Casts briefly leave fading starlike footsteps. Ending visions gain a full magical aura. The render layer uses procedural geometry, instancing, shader-soft motes and controlled original arpeggios, without new lights or external assets. A room holds at most two performances, one thread and 24 fading footsteps. Scene transitions dispose casts; pause and hidden tabs stop updates. Reduced motion freezes orbits, pollen motion and unfurling, and suppresses footprints. Generated audio uses the existing controlled bus, so mute and hidden-tab cancellation apply.

Validation includes storage and duplicate protection, safe positions, bounded draw objects and disposal, eight-flower progression, reveal/collect lifecycle, reduced motion, and audio cancellation. The browser suite renders all three spells and compares pixels with/without each performance, collects a mark using the actual E interaction, reloads the saved page, checks pause/scene cleanup and inspects a mobile grimoire. Existing scene, quest and scare suites remain in the matrix. Software WebGL does not establish hardware FPS or replace a child playtest.
