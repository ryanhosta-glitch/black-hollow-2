# A house with a point of view

This pass ties original music, the emotional turn and the player's immediate next move together. It does not quote a film score, imitate an actor's voice, or use celebrity assets.

## Original adaptive score

Each of the eight haunting families has its own original eight-note contour, register, tempo and chamber-synth timbre. Sparse phrases leave the second half of a musical cycle largely empty. Higher pressure adds a higher register and a restrained pulse; a revelation changes the motif's direction and an ending changes its harmony. The instrument combines a sine or triangle fundamental with a soft octave partial and stereo placement.

The generated score replaces the recorded background track while exploring an authored room in The Unwritten Night. Classic investigations, the physical Hall organ, the street and Trick or Treat keep their existing music. Puzzle bells and replayed choir phrases suppress both the adaptive layer and recorded backgrounds for a listening window, including fallback Hall encounters. The layer pauses with menus, does not accumulate a burst while paused, cancels on hidden/mute, and releases on scene changes. Up to twelve sustained score oscillators are tracked, with brief fading tails during a transition. This is synthesized chamber music, not a sampled orchestral recording.

## Theatre and revelation

Each house introduces itself with an original uncanny image, an immediate plain-language next move, and a dry theatrical aside. The intro starts at its heading rather than scrolling the reader to the final button.

A first revelation now has a dedicated nonblocking story card and a moment of musical space. Its original thematic line reframes the existing reveal. It can be dismissed; it does not move the camera or stop the player's control. A saved encounter marker prevents repeated presentation in that run. Changing scenes dismisses the card. Reduced-motion users receive static presentation.

## Lantern guide

The optional guide gives one practical next instruction and a location. A physical golden ring marks the target only in the player's current room, using normal depth testing. It does not draw a route through walls or replace the architect's doors, stairs and elevators. It never names the next correct choir note, engraved clock or disguised guest. Those puzzles still require reading, remembering and comparing clues. Experienced players can turn the guide off from the status panel or journal. The preference persists on this browser.

Validation includes motif uniqueness and musical bounds; pause and voice budgets; guide spoiler boundaries; saved preference; actual-scene haunting flows; and a Chromium/WebGL suite for audible score-node creation, choir listening windows, guide rings, a one-time turning point, mute, mobile overflow and screenshots. Device performance and subjective musical quality still need in-person playtesting.
